Q1 (Summary):
Briefly summarize the paper and its contributions. Do not critique the paper here. The authors should agree with a well-written summary. Typical range: 3-8 sentences.
The paper introduces F2D2, a joint distillation framework that enables both few-step sampling and few-step log-likelihood evaluation in continuous normalizing flow/flow-matching models by learning a single joint flow map for the coupled ODEs of state evolution and log-density change (Eq. 2.3; Sec. 3.1). The model shares a backbone and adds a divergence head to predict time-averaged divergence alongside time-averaged velocity, with validity enforced via flow-map conditions; two concrete instantiations are derived: Shortcut-F2D2 using the semigroup property (Algorithm 1; Sec. 3.2.1) and MeanFlow-F2D2 using the Eulerian condition (Sec. 3.2.2). Practical training employs Hutchinson’s trace estimator and staged warm starts, including a Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 variant that leverages a teacher for stable supervision (Sec. 3.3). As an application, the paper proposes maximum-likelihood self-guidance that optimizes the initial noise using the fast likelihood head with a single backward pass (Algorithm 2; Sec. 3.4). Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64 show calibrated NLLs close to teacher BPDs while maintaining competitive FIDs with 1–8 NFEs (Tables 1–2; Fig. 1–2), and accurate few-step likelihoods on a 2D checkerboard benchmark (Fig. 3). Notably, MeanFlow-F2D2 with self-guidance enables a 2-step model to surpass the FID of a 1024-step flow-matching teacher of the same size on CIFAR-10 (Sec. 5.3).
Q2 (Soundness):
Please assign the paper a numerical rating on the following scale to indicate the soundness of the technical claims, experimental and research methodology and on whether the central claims of the paper are adequately supported with evidence: 4 excellent, 3 good, 2 fair, 1 poor.
The paper’s technical core is well-grounded: the joint flow-map parameterization for the coupled state/likelihood ODEs is derived from established flow-map conditions and is formalized via Proposition 3.3 with concrete Eulerian/semigroup instantiations (Sec. 3.1–3.2; App. A–B). The training objectives for both velocity and divergence follow directly from these identities, and Algorithm 1 provides a clear implementation of the shortcut-based variant. Empirically, evidence supports the central claim that few-step likelihoods can be made practical while retaining high sample quality. On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64, F2D2 variants turn previously invalid few-step NLLs into calibrated, positive values and often near the teacher BPD at moderate NFE (Tables 1–2). The per-sample NLL error drops by large factors compared to baselines (Table 7). The 2D checkerboard with ground-truth likelihood shows accurate recovery even at 1 step (Fig. 3). Sample quality is competitive, and the self-guidance application demonstrates additional utility (Fig. 2). Caveats: NLL calibration drifts at very low NFEs for some variants (e.g., MeanFlow-F2D2 at 1–2 steps in Table 1 and Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 at 1–2 steps in Table 2), indicating residual bias in the integral approximation. Training relies on sensitive divergence-target scaling and early stopping (Sec. 3.3; App. D; Sec. F), and some configurations leverage a teacher’s divergence for stability (App. C), which partly weakens claims of teacher-free generality. Statistical reporting lacks error bars. Overall, the methodology is sound and the evidence largely supports the main claims, with some limitations at extreme few-step regimes and sensitivity to hyperparameters. Rating: 3

The paper tackles an important, under-addressed bottleneck: tractable, few-step likelihoods for diffusion/flow models. The joint flow-map formulation for coupled state and log-density ODEs is clear and original in its execution, with concrete shortcut/Eulerian instantiations (Sec. 3.1–3.2; Algorithm 1; Prop. 3.3). Results convincingly show calibrated few-step NLLs alongside strong sample quality on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64 (Tables 1–2; Fig. 1–2) and exact recovery on a ground-truth 2D benchmark (Fig. 3); the self-guidance application underscores utility (Sec. 5.3). Limitations include sensitivity to divergence scaling and early stopping, partial reliance on teacher supervision in some settings, and occasional degradation at extreme low NFEs (Sec. 3.3; App. D; Sec. F; Tables 1–2). Overall, this is a substantial and timely contribution of broad interest. Rating: 3

1. Clear, principled formulation of a joint flow map for sampling and log-likelihood. The paper elevates the coupled ODEs (Eq. 2.3) to a single operator with shared backbone and two heads (Eq. 3.3), then rigorously characterizes validity via Lagrangian/Eulerian/semigroup conditions (Proposition 3.3; Appendix A). This unifies disparate accelerations under a common, verifiable framework. 2. Concrete, well-derived instantiations. The shortcut variant translates the semigroup into explicit self-consistency for both velocity and divergence with implementable losses (Eqs. 3.5–3.10; Algorithm 1). The MeanFlow variant directly enforces the Eulerian relation for divergence analogously to velocity (Eqs. 3.11–3.13). These are not cosmetic extensions; they mirror the core identities governing the joint system. 3. New theoretical clarification for MeanFlow. Corollary B.2 proves that the MeanFlow identity enforces the Eulerian condition (Appendix B), tightening the theoretical foundation of a widely used few-step sampler. 4. Minimal architectural overhead with strong modularity. The divergence head is just a lightweight scalar predictor attached to an existing flow-map backbone (Sec. 3.1, 3.3). This makes F2D2 plug-and-play with Shortcut and MeanFlow without redesigning training pipelines. 5. Careful treatment of divergence supervision, a genuine pain point. The paper combines Hutchinson’s estimator, staged warm starts, and optional teacher divergence to stabilize learning (Sec. 3.3; Appendix C). The ablation on divergence scaling (Table 5) is unusually transparent and practically useful. 6. Strong empirical evidence that few-step likelihoods can be calibrated while preserving sample quality. On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64, F2D2 variants convert the baselines’ invalid, large-magnitude negative NLLs into positive, calibrated BPDs while keeping competitive FIDs across 1–8 NFEs (Tables 1–2; Fig. 1–2). This directly addresses the central bottleneck the paper targets. 7. Ground-truth validation beyond image benchmarks. On the 2D checkerboard with analytic likelihood, Shortcut-F2D2 accurately reconstructs the density even at 1 NFE whereas baselines collapse (Fig. 3), isolating and confirming the likelihood-accuracy claim. 8. Per-sample likelihood accuracy, not just aggregate BPD. The mean absolute per-sample BPD error relative to the teacher drops by large factors for all F2D2 variants (Table 7), indicating practical reliability for likelihood-based downstream uses. 9. Demonstrated new capability enabled by fast likelihood: self-guidance. The proposed maximum-likelihood self-guidance (Algorithm 2) lets a 2-step MeanFlow-F2D2 surpass a 1024-step flow-matching teacher of the same size on CIFAR-10 (Sec. 5.3; Fig. 2), showcasing immediate algorithmic utility of the added head. 10. Reproducibility and transparency. The paper includes proofs (Appendices A–B), complete implementation details and hyperparameters (Appendix C; Table 3), speed/latency measurements (Table 4), ablations on key sensitivities (Appendix D; Tables 5–6), training dynamics (Fig. 5), and extensive non-cherry-picked samples (Appendix E), making the work straightforward to replicate and extend. 11. Practical efficiency gains are quantified. The reported wall-clock times and per-image latencies across NFEs (Table 4) substantiate the claim of two orders of magnitude fewer NFEs for both sampling and likelihood without trading away performance.

Weaknesses (with actionable suggestions) 1. Reliance on teacher calibration and supervision undermines generality - Evidence: Staged Training with Warm Start and Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 use teacher velocity/divergence for supervision (Sec. 3.3; Appendix C: “pre-train a teacher flow matching model vϕ… replacing div(uθ) with div(vϕ)”). Early stopping explicitly targets the teacher BPD (“stop the training when the calibrated value of bpd is reached”, App. C). - Why it matters: The central claim is fast, accurate likelihood from a distilled few-step model. If accuracy critically depends on a high-NFE teacher and tuning to its BPD, this weakens claims of teacher-free applicability and real-likelihood fidelity. - Suggestions: 1) Provide teacher-free ablations at scale (CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64) that train Dθ solely via self-consistency/Hutchinson and report NLL/FID (Tables 1–2). 2) Report how NLL accuracy degrades as teacher quality or teacher-NFE is reduced. 3) Replace “BPD match to teacher” early stopping by a validation objective independent of the teacher (e.g., path-consistency residuals from Eq. A.12/A.19, or a held-out convergence metric across Kll). 2. Sensitivity to divergence scaling and early stopping threatens robustness and reproducibility - Evidence: Necessity of large target scaling factors and early stopping (Sec. 3.3; App. D Table 5 shows strong sensitivity; Sec. F: “requires careful early stopping” and sensitivity to scaling). - Why it matters: Heavy sensitivity suggests brittle training and risks miscalibrated NLL outside the tuned regime. - Suggestions: 1) Normalize Dθ targets online (e.g., per-batch standardization or running-mean/var) to remove reliance on fixed scalars (App. D). 2) Regularize Dθ via consistency across nested intervals (additional midpoints beyond r=(t+s)/2) and multi-noise Hutchinson averaging per step to reduce variance. 3) Provide a schedule for λ in Eq. D.1 with validation curves to automate early stopping without teacher BPD (App. D Table 6 hints this is feasible). 3. NLL calibration drifts at very small NFEs; error sources not analyzed - Evidence: On CIFAR-10, MeanFlow-F2D2 at 1–2 steps yields BPD far from the teacher (e.g., 1.29 vs 3.12 at 2 steps, Table 1). Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 also deviates at 1–2 steps on ImageNet 64×64 (Table 2). - Why it matters: The headline claim is “few-step” likelihood; the most compelling regime is Kll ≤ 2–4. Systematic underestimation at low K indicates residual bias in the integral approximation of ∫div(v)dt. - Suggestions: 1) Report NLL bias vs K in log–log plots and decompose error into flow-map error (uθ) vs divergence-map error (Dθ) by plugging each into Eq. 2.4 separately. 2) Add losses that couple Dθ to uθ via integral constraints over random (t,s) (e.g., check Eq. A.7/A.12 over multiple s per t in one minibatch). 3) Evaluate alternative time parameterizations (importance-weighted sampling of (t,s)) to reduce high-curvature bias at endpoints. 4. Fairness of baselines for likelihood is unclear; F2D2 adds capacity/objective absent in baselines - Evidence: Baseline Shortcut/MeanFlow “can, in principle, evaluate likelihood” via uθ(x,t,t) and Hutchinson, but are shown with “invalid NLL” (App. C). They were never trained to predict divergence or to satisfy the Z-flow conditions used by F2D2 (Sec. 3.2). - Why it matters: The improvement might partly come from adding and training an explicit divergence head/objective. - Suggestions: 1) Train baselines with a divergence head but without joint flow-map constraints (no L_D terms), keeping capacity the same, to isolate the contribution of F2D2’s joint constraints. 2) Compare against the closest likelihood-capable few-step method you cite (e.g., Kim et al. (2023), Sec. 1/4) under matched NFE-quality/runtime, even if that method needs more Kll, to position F2D2 on the Pareto frontier. 5. Limited validation of “likelihood correctness” beyond teacher agreement - Evidence: On images, correctness is mainly judged by closeness to teacher BPD (Tables 1–2), plus per-sample error vs teacher (Table 7). Ground-truth validation appears only on 2D checkerboard (Fig. 3). - Why it matters: Teacher likelihoods are themselves approximations. Agreement with the teacher is not proof of correctness. - Suggestions: 1) For a subset of images, compute high-accuracy probability-flow ODE likelihoods with stringent tolerances and multiple solvers to establish a more solver-independent reference. 2) Report solver-sensitivity sweeps (tolerances/NFEs) for the teacher likelihoods to bound reference error. 3) Include calibration plots (predicted vs reference per-sample BPD; reliability curves) in addition to MAE (Table 7). 6. Inference-time speed reporting excludes likelihood evaluation and joint use-cases - Evidence: Table 4 reports sampling throughput only; the central contribution is fast likelihood and sampling. - Why it matters: Users will often need both sample and likelihood together (e.g., RLHF-like objectives, filtering). - Suggestions: 1) Report wall-clock for joint sample+likelihood under shared forward passes for K_samp and K_ll, including backward cost for self-guidance (Algorithm 2). 2) Provide a runtime–quality Pareto (FID vs time; BPD error vs time) for F2D2 vs the teacher and a strong baseline. 7. Self-guidance evaluation focuses on FID improvements but omits likelihood/distributional effects - Evidence: Sec. 5.3 and Fig. 2 show FID gains; no analysis of how self-guidance affects likelihood calibration or coverage. - Why it matters: Optimizing x0 by maximizing model likelihood might distort generated distribution or miscalibrate Dθ. - Suggestions: 1) Report post-guidance BPD of generated samples, compare to pre-guidance, and show whether guidance preserves/harms calibration. 2) Assess mode coverage metrics or diversity proxies alongside FID. 3) Vary the number of guidance steps and learning rates with NLL accuracy tracked (App. D Fig. 4 shows FID only). 8. Scope of evaluation is narrow relative to claims of generality - Evidence: Only unconditional CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64×64 are presented; limitations acknowledge reduced model size/compute constraints (Sec. F). - Why it matters: Claims of modularity and broad compatibility (Sec. 3.1–3.2) would be strengthened by breadth. - Suggestions: 1) Add at least one conditional image benchmark (e.g., class-conditional ImageNet 64×64) to test whether Dθ remains calibrated under conditioning inputs. 2) Demonstrate on a non-image or higher-resolution setting, or a score-SDE probability-flow ODE, to show portability. 9. Theoretical guarantees do not translate into quantitative diagnostics during training - Evidence: Joint validity is characterized via Lagrangian/Eulerian/semigroup conditions (Prop. 3.3; App. A), but there are no reported diagnostics showing how well these constraints are satisfied during training/inference beyond losses (Fig. 5 shows only loss curves). - Why it matters: Without constraint satisfaction diagnostics, it is hard to know if observed NLL errors stem from violating Eq. A.12/A.19 vs estimator noise. - Suggestions: 1) Track and report residuals of Eq. A.12 and Eq. A.19 on held-out minibatches over training; correlate with BPD error vs K. 2) Provide qualitative examples where residuals are large and analyze failure modes. 10. Occasional degradation in sample quality when maximizing NLL accuracy - Evidence: On CIFAR-10, Shortcut-F2D2 achieves calibrated NLL but with worse FID than MeanFlow/Shortcut variants at small K (Table 1); on ImageNet 64×64, Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 improves NLL but FID trails Shortcut-Distill (Table 2). - Why it matters: The stated goal is to maintain high sample quality while enabling fast likelihood. - Suggestions: 1) Explore multi-objective training schedules (as hinted by App. D Table 6) and per-loss adaptive weights to balance L_VM/L_u with L_div/L_D dynamically based on validation FID and BPD error. 2) Investigate architectural decoupling (e.g., partial parameter sharing or FiLM conditioning) to let Dθ improve without harming uθ.
